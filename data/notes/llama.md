# Meta Llama (1, 2, 3, 4): how experiments became final runs

Numbers flagged `reported` were read verbatim from those mirrors/cards; `derived` numbers state the formula.

## 1. Final runs

**Llama 3 (paper Jul 31 2024; models Apr 18 and Jul 23 2024).** Three dense models: 8B, 70B, 405B, all pre-trained on ~15T tokens (flagship: 15.6T). The flagship budget is stated as 3.8e25 FLOPs, "almost 50x more than the largest version of Llama 2"; 6*N*D with N=405e9, D=15.6e12 reproduces 3.79e25. GPU-hours come from the Llama 3.1 model card, not the paper: 1.46M (8B), 7.0M (70B), 30.84M (405B), total 39.3M H100-80GB hours, 11,390 tCO2eq location-based (https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama3_1/MODEL_CARD.md). The April 2024 Llama 3 card had listed 1.3M/6.4M (total 7.7M) for 8B/70B; Meta does not explain the ~0.16M/0.6M increase in the 3.1 card (presumably long-context continued training). Hardware: up to 16,384 H100 (700W, 80GB HBM3) in Grand Teton servers, on a 24K-GPU RoCE cluster (three-layer Clos, 400 Gbps, 1:7 oversubscription at the top layer); smaller models on a Quantum InfiniBand cluster. Meta's March 12 2024 engineering blog says two 24K-GPU clusters were being used for Llama 3 training (https://engineering.fb.com/2024/03/12/data-center-engineering/building-metas-genai-infrastructure/), which bounds the pre-training period (start before Mar 2024; 70B/405B knowledge cutoff Dec 2023; release Jul 23 2024). MFU (Table 4): 43% at 8,192 GPUs (430 TFLOPs/GPU), 41% at 16,384 GPUs (400 TFLOPs/GPU), 38% for the 128K-context phase (380 TFLOPs/GPU); tokens/batch 16M throughout. Reliability: in a 54-day snapshot, 466 interruptions (47 planned, 419 unexpected; 78% hardware, 58.7% GPU), >90% effective training time. Recipe: AdamW peak LR 8e-5, 8,000 warmup steps, cosine to 8e-7 over 1.2M steps; batch 4M tokens@4096 -> 8M@8192 after 252M tokens -> 16M after 2.87T tokens; "few loss spikes ... no interventions". Then ~800B tokens of long-context training in six stages (8K->128K), then 40M tokens of annealing to LR 0 with Polyak averaging. Cross-check: 30.84M GPU-h x 3600 s x 400 TFLOPs = 4.4e25 peak-rate FLOPs vs 3.8e25 stated (86%), consistent with MFU-adjusted throughput and >90% effective time. Epoch AI derives ~72 days of training at 380 TFLOPs (https://x.com/EpochAIResearch/status/1815778832265232483). No numeric training/validation loss for any final model is printed in the paper. Pre-trained benchmarks (paper Tables 12-13): MMLU 66.7/79.3/85.2, GSM8K 57.2/83.7/89.0, MATH 20.3/41.4/53.8, ARC-C 79.7/92.9/96.1 for 8B/70B/405B. Post-training: six rounds of SFT + rejection sampling + DPO; compute not stated. Author list: 559 on arXiv (search snippet; ResearchGate lists 531); the paper separates "core contributors" (>=2/3 of project runtime) from "contributors" (>=1/5).

**Llama 2 (Jul 18 2023, 68 authors).** 7B/13B/34B/70B, all on 2.0T tokens, 4k context, batch 4M tokens; A100-80GB on the Research Super Cluster (InfiniBand, 400W cap) and production clusters (RoCE, 350W cap), 200 Gbps. GPU-hours (paper Table 2 = model card): 184,320 / 368,640 / 1,038,336 / 1,720,320, total 3,311,616, 539 tCO2eq. Trained "between January 2023 and July 2023" (model card). Loss curves (Figure 5) show "no sign of saturation" at 2T tokens, figure only. Pre-trained MMLU 45.3/54.8/62.6/68.9. Chat models: 27,540 SFT annotations, 1,418,091 Meta preference comparisons, RLHF-V1..V5. The 34B was trained but not released.

**LLaMA 1 (arXiv Feb 27 2023, 14 authors).** 6.7B/13.0B on 1.0T tokens, 32.5B/65.2B on 1.4T tokens, batch 4M, 2048 A100-80GB. GPU-hours (Table 15): 82,432 / 135,168 / 530,432 / 1,022,362 (sum 1,770,394). 65B ran at 380 tokens/s/GPU on 2048 A100s, ~21 days (21 x 2048 x 24 = 1.03M GPU-h, consistent). MMLU 35.1/46.9/57.8/63.4 (as restated in the Llama 2 paper).

**Llama 4 (blog Apr 5 2025; no technical report).** Scout 109B total/17B active/16 experts, ~40T tokens, 5.0M H100-hours; Maverick 400B/17B/128 experts, ~22T tokens, 2.38M H100-hours (total 7.38M, 1,999 tCO2eq; https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama4/MODEL_CARD.md). Behemoth (288B active, 16 experts, ~2T total) was "still training" and unreleased; the blog says FP8 pre-training on 32K GPUs reached 390 TFLOPs/GPU, and the overall mixture exceeded 30T tokens, "more than double the Llama 3 pre-training mixture" (https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-4-multimodal-intelligence/, confirmed via mirrors and search snippets). Behemoth GPU-hours and any Llama 4 experiment compute are not disclosed.

## 2. Experimental runs (Llama 3)

*IsoFLOP ladder (Section 3.2.1).* Models of 40M-16B parameters at compute budgets from 6e18 to 1e22 FLOPs, "using a subset of model sizes at each compute budget"; the number of runs is not stated. Ladder hyperparameters: 2,000-step warmup, peak LR 2e-4 to 4e-4, cosine to 0.1x, weight decay 0.1 x LR, fixed batch per scale (250K-4M tokens). Loss is NLL on a held-out validation set; per-budget parabolas (second-degree polynomials) give compute-optimal points (Figure 2, figure only). Figure 2's axis shows budgets 1e19, 3e19, 6e19, 1e20, 3e20, 6e20, 1e21, 3e21, 1e22 plus the 6e18 named in text (read off the figure). Fit N*(C) = A C^alpha with (alpha, A) = (0.53, 0.29) in the text; Figure 3's legend reads 0.537/0.299. Extrapolation to 3.8e25 gives 402B params on 16.55T tokens; the team chose 405B because IsoFLOP minima flatten with scale. Summing the visible budgets gives ~1.5e22 per model-per-budget; even 6 models per budget would be ~9e22 FLOPs, ~0.2% of the flagship (a guess - the count is not given).

*Two-stage benchmark prediction.* Stage 1: linear fit of normalized NLL-per-character of the correct answer vs log FLOPs, using only ladder models <=1e22. Stage 2: sigmoid from NLL to accuracy fitted on ladder models plus Llama 2 7B-70B (trained on Llama 2 data/tokenizer). Shown for ARC Challenge (Figure 4, FLOPs axis 1e20-1e25); coefficients and the predicted vs actual 405B accuracy are not printed. "Extrapolates over four orders of magnitude ... only slightly underestimates the final performance."

*Data mix (Section 3.1.2).* Knowledge classifier to downsample over-represented web categories, plus "scaling law experiments in which we train several small models on a data mix and use that to predict the performance of a large model on that mix ... repeat this process multiple times for different data mixes ... then train a larger model on this candidate". No sizes, counts or compute. Result: ~50% general knowledge, 25% math/reasoning, 17% code, 8% multilingual. The mix was further adjusted during the flagship run (more non-English, more math, fresher web data, downsampled low-quality subsets).

*Annealing ablations (Section 3.1.3 / 3.4.3).* (a) Annealing on GSM8k/MATH training sets improved a pre-trained 8B by 24.0% (GSM8k) and 6.4% (MATH) but gave "negligible" gains on 405B. (b) Data-quality probe: anneal a 50%-trained 8B linearly to LR 0 over 40B tokens with 30% weight on the candidate dataset (~1.9e21 FLOPs per probe, derived); the number of probes is not stated. Meta says this is cheaper than a scaling-law experiment per dataset.

*Branch runs.* A code expert was made by "branching the main pre-training run and continuing pre-training on a 1T token mix of mostly (>85%) code"; a multilingual expert by branching and continuing on 90% multilingual tokens. Model sizes and compute unstated.

*Llama 1/2/4.* No ladder or ablation runs are described for Llama 2 pre-training (only the size series and loss curves; RLHF picked "among several ablations at each iteration" via reward models). LLaMA 1 publishes only loss curves but does give total development compute (below). Llama 4 describes MetaP hyperparameter transfer and codistillation qualitatively with no numbers.

## 3. Lab level and experiments-vs-final compute

Llama 3 states nothing about how much compute went to experiments versus final runs; the 39.3M GPU-hours are per-model "training time" figures and the flagship budget is given only for the final run. Llama 2 and Llama 4 are likewise silent. LLaMA 1 is the exception: "we estimate that we used 2048 A100-80GB for a period of approximately 5 months to develop our models ... around 2,638 MWh ... 1,015 tCO2eq". At the paper's own 400W x PUE 1.1, 2,638 MWh is ~6.0M GPU-hours, of which the four released runs account for 1.77M (29.5%); roughly 70% of Llama 1 development compute therefore went to non-final runs (derived). Headcounts: 14 (Llama 1), 68 (Llama 2), 559 arXiv authors (Llama 3), unstated (Llama 4). Cluster growth: 2,048 A100 -> RSC + production A100 -> 24K-GPU H100 clusters (16K used) -> 32K GPUs for Behemoth; Meta planned 350K H100s by end-2024.

## Verification status (unconfirmed or weakly confirmed)

- All arXiv paper quotes were checked against GitHub-hosted text conversions, not the canonical PDFs (arxiv.org blocked). The Llama 3 mirror is a PDF-to-markdown conversion; figures/tables (notably post-trained Table 2) were partially garbled, so post-trained benchmark numbers were deliberately not recorded.
- Llama 3 author count 559 (arXiv) vs 531 (ResearchGate): from search snippets only; the mirror's contributor lines (~222 core contributors, 62 contributors) may be truncated.
- Llama 4 blog wording ("390 TFLOPs/GPU", "32K GPUs", ">30 trillion tokens") confirmed via three GitHub mirrors and a search snippet, not the live page; whether ">30T" applies to Behemoth alone is unclear.
- Meta engineering blog (two 24K clusters used for Llama 3) and Epoch AI's ~72-day estimate: search snippets only.
- Figure-derived items (IsoFLOP budget list; Figure 3 legend 0.537/0.299) come from axis text in the converted PDF and should be re-read from the original figure.
- Cost estimates use an unsourced $2.00/H100-hour and $1.50/A100-hour assumption (guess); no price source was consulted because the web-search budget ran out.
- Llama 3 pre-training start/end dates are not published anywhere found; only bounds (training underway by 2024-03-12; release 2024-07-23) and the 54-day snapshot.
- Whether the 800B long-context tokens are included in the 15.6T flagship total is not stated.
- Llama 3.3 70B card repeats 7.0M GPU-hours (same as 3.1 70B) with only 12.9 tCO2eq, implying the 7.0M is inherited from 3.1 rather than new compute; not confirmed by Meta.
