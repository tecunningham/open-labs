# Marin (Stanford CRFM / Open Athena) — how experiments became final runs

Collected 2026-09-13. Access constraints: marin.community, huggingface.co, marin.readthedocs.io, openathena.ai, wandb.ai, x.com, arxiv.org and the Google blogs were blocked by the egress proxy. Everything below comes from (a) the Marin GitHub repo (retrospectives in `docs/reports/`, `README.md`, experiment code, issue pages, the Jekyll source of the announcement post), (b) the public `gs://marin-public/index.json`, and (c) search-engine snippets of the blocked pages, which are flagged as such. Numbers quoted from a snippet rather than a page I could read are marked (snippet).

## 1. What the lab is and how it runs experiments

Marin launched publicly on 2025-05-19 as "an open lab" from Stanford CRFM/HAI, with 22 authors on the announcement (Hall … Liang) (https://github.com/marin-community/marin-community.github.io/blob/main/_posts/2025-05-19-announcement.md, mirror of https://marin.community/blog/2025/05/19/announcement/). The stated method: every experiment is a GitHub issue that acts as a mini-preregistration (hypothesis, plan, results), the experiment itself is a PR of declarative Python (`experiments/exp<issue>_*.py`), training goes to W&B, and results are written back to the issue. The announcement explicitly bifurcates work into (1) controlled small-scale ablations and (2) "a few YOLO runs which eventually led to our best models", with occasional controlled experiments "peeled off" from the YOLO run.

Scale of the experimental record: GitHub search returns **446 issues with the `experiment` label** as of 2026-09-13 (https://github.com/search?q=repo%3Amarin-community%2Fmarin+label%3Aexperiment&type=issues); issue/PR numbering has passed #9126. The hand-curated "Selected Experiment Reports" page (https://github.com/marin-community/marin/blob/main/docs/reports/index.md) lists roughly 50 experiments with W&B links across cooldowns, modeling, training/perf, data, SFT, scaling laws and baselines. A separate machine-generated digest covers **80 "Agent MoE" sub-issues** run between 2026-03-31 and 2026-08-20, with outcomes 16 worked / 9 promising / 13 mixed / 32 did not work / 6 not evaluated / 4 in progress (https://github.com/marin-community/marin/blob/main/docs/reports/agent-moe-experiments.md). A per-user "publish an analysis site" mechanism (GCS bucket `marin-public`) is used for post-training/RL studies in 2026.

Funding and hardware: the announcement says "Nearly all of the compute used for Marin comes from TRC" (Google TPU Research Cloud). The README (https://github.com/marin-community/marin/blob/main/README.md) now lists supporters: Google TRC (TPUs), the Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang Foundation (GPU clusters), Siegel Family Endowment and Schmidt Sciences (development). Team size: the Open Athena 535B launch note states Marin grew "from one FTE to ten" since joining Open Athena, and that a year earlier it had one FTE (snippet, https://openathena.ai/blog/marin-535b-launch-note/). Google's Dec-2025 post says one researcher kept the 32B run alive all summer while mostly working on other things (snippet, https://opensource.googleblog.com/2025/12/training-marin-32b-what-an-open-lab-can-build-with-tpus-jax-and-a-little-persistence.html).

## 2. The final runs

**Marin 8B "Tootsie" (exp600).** Llama-3.1-8B architecture, 12.7T tokens, released 2025-05-19 (https://github.com/marin-community/marin/blob/main/docs/reports/marin-8b-retro.md). Hardware: 2x TPU v5e-256 multislice for the first ~2.7T tokens, then a reserved v4-2048. The run had no pre-planned recipe ("we didn't fully know the best recipe — so we just started training with what we had"). Phases were named retrospectively: Kestrel (DCLM mix, WSD-S, 0→2.7T), Ocelot (v4-2048, batch 4Mi→12Mi, LR 1e-3→1.7e-3, WSD+EMA, 2.7→3.78T), Jellyfish (first cooldown to 1.7e-4 with 70/30 HQ mix, →4.78T; MMLU 65.3), Phoenix (rewarm, switch to Nemotron-CC, →11.1T), Starling (cooldown to 1.7e-5 with z-loss 1e-4, batch 16Mi, 1.34T tokens), Deeper Starling (coast at 1.7e-5, →12.7T). Final base: 19-task average 66.6 vs Llama 3.1 8B 65.3, MMLU 67.6; beats Llama 3.1 8B on 14/19. SFT (~5B tokens) produced Marin 8B Instruct (avg 43.8). Derived FLOPs ≈ 6·8.03e9·1.27e13 ≈ 6.1e23. Side experiments that fed decisions: microannealing runs (#784, #820) that found 70% PT / 15% FLAN / 15% HQ best and that HQ data alone hurts task performance; "dessert" patch runs; Raccoon (#898) and Spoonbill (#916) deep-cooldown runs that isolated an `lm_head` norm explosion and led to z-loss.

**Marin 32B "Mantis" (exp1295 → exp1395 → exp1529).** Issue opened 2025-05-18; training declared done 2025-10-29 (Percy Liang tweet 1983561556127567911). Retro: https://github.com/marin-community/marin/blob/main/docs/reports/marin-32b-retro.md. Started on 4 preemptible v5p-512 slices, dropped to 3 at step 18,500, moved to a reserved v4-2048 at step 21,010. Phase 1 (Llama-3-style 32B, OLMo-2-32B dims, AdamW 7e-4, batch 32Mi, 80k steps, 2.679T tokens) had persistent loss spikes; grad-norm clipping to 0.2, clip-update-norm and skip-bad-steps softened but did not fix them (#1368). Phase 2 rescue attempts — optimizer-state "necromancy" (#1390) and a Muon swap (#1380) — failed and were discarded. Phase 3 warm-started a Qwen3-32B backbone (adds QK-Norm) from the 80k Llama weights; loss recovered in ~10B tokens and spikes disappeared (80k→160k, 2.684T). Phase 4a "Bison" cooldown (160k→192k, 1.074T) was strong except GSM8K, traced to Dolmino `test.json` contamination cached on the cluster, plus a training-loss phase shift traced to the LCG/affine data permutation; 4b "Mantis" (Feistel shuffle, MegaMath) reran the same 1.074T and was released. Final artifact 6.437T tokens (derived FLOPs ≈ 1.24e24); one full cooldown (~2.1e23 FLOPs) was discarded. Results: 19-task avg 65.2, MMLU 74.7, MMLU-Pro 45.1, BBH 59.6; beats OLMo 2 32B on 14/19, roughly ties Gemma 3 27B PT.

**Other pretraining runs.** Tootsie 13B (#860), 24B (#861) and 70B (#750) on preemptible v6e multislice were opened Jan–Mar 2025 and closed Aug 2025 with empty results sections (the 32B retro mentions an "earlier trial 70B run" that was stable). **Snowball 67B-A2B** (#6044, opened 2026-05-29): "overtrained" Grug MoE pre+midtrained on 10T tokens, extended to 262K context, with a post-training pipeline; a 2.7T-token checkpoint was used for RL studies in Aug 2026 (snippet + https://storage.googleapis.com/marin-public/index.json). **535B-A23B hero run** (#8435, opened 2026-08-18; started the week of 2026-08-21): 18.75T tokens, 80% pretraining + 20% midtraining, 11 x GB200 NVL72 for ~3 months, 2.7e24 FLOPs, 384 routed experts top-8 plus 2 shared, context 4k→65k→262k, compute gifted by the Huang Foundation (https://x.com/percyliang/status/2090918065634684997, https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/8435). Derived plan: 792 GPUs × ~90 d × 24 h ≈ 1.7e6 GPU-hours.

## 3. Scaling laws and ladders

Marin's 2025 flagship runs were *not* preceded by a published ladder. Issue #654 ("Scaling laws to predict tootsie performance", a "PoC") and #780 (planned 5-model ladders per data method, projecting to 8B/13B/22B) have empty or absent results in the accessible text; #621 concluded MuP "not worth it compared to our heuristic version". The 8B retro chose 8B "mostly out of pragmatism" (reserved capacity), and the 32B was "largely a scale-up of the 8B recipe".

That changed in 2026:
- **Delphi** (issue #1337 opened 2025-05-22; released 2026-05-11): a "modernized Pythia" scaling suite. Recipe = Qwen3 architecture + AdamH "Completed" heuristic (LR ∝ √B·(T0/T)^0.3, β2 constant-token-half-life, etc.; https://github.com/marin-community/marin/blob/main/experiments/references/completed_adamh.py). The IsoFLOP grid hard-coded in `reference_scaling_suite.py` has 78 runs at seven budgets 3e18…3e20 (d512–d4096); optima at 1e21/1e22/1e23 were trained as held-out targets (3 seeds at 1e21/1e22). A pre-registered forecast fit on the seven IsoFLOP optima predicted the 1e23 run (25B params, 600B tokens) within 0.2%, extrapolating 300x past the fit; the ladder cost ~1e21 FLOPs (snippets of https://openathena.ai/blog/delphi/, README). The fitting code (`isoflop_analysis.py`) fits a Huber-robust quadratic in log10(tokens) per budget and then D* ~ A·C^α (Chinchilla approach 2); the fitted α is not in any text I could read. 88 checkpoints plus a plot-data dataset were released.
- **MoE ladders.** v16 AdamH MoE IsoFLOP sweep (#4447, 1e18–1e20) gave loss(C)=1.6+95.18·C^-0.0941 with L∞ pinned at 1.6, validated at 1e21 (predicted 2.606 vs measured 2.599) and used to size a 1e23 run (~131B/16B-active, ~1.02T tokens; #4281 milestone "1e23 (120B-A12B-ish) compute optimal launched + pre-registered"). The "May Recipe" (MuonH + quantile-balancing router) refit gives 1.6+88.3–88.9·C^-0.0941, a ~2.1–2.2x equal-throughput speedup (#6074, `experiments/grug/moe/README.md`). MoE tests at 1e21 (14B-A2B, 75B tokens) and 1e22 (35B-A5B, 326B tokens, v4-512) validated quantile balancing (#3800). A MuonH LR law (LR = 26.9·tokens^-0.395·dim^-0.121·√B, R²=0.996 on 17 of ~150 runs, #5951) removed the need for per-size sweeps.
- **535B ladder.** A 4-rung "hero-shape" ladder from 1.6B-A61M (48B tokens) to 27.7B-A1.2B (926B tokens) was trained before launch to debug and forecast; #8435 states the ladder "only costs 1% of total compute". The forecast loss value is in a W&B report I could not open.
- **Agent MoE gate.** Ideas are screened at d512–d1280 against the scaling-law baseline; "effective speedup" is computed by inverting the fitted law and multiplying by measured throughput, and survivors go up the ladder (agent-moe digest; `docs/recipes/add_scaling_heuristic.md` codifies: tune at ~130M/2.5B tokens → define scaling rules → IsoFLOP sweep → ladder → promote to a daily ~30M canary).

## 4. What they said they learned

From the 8B retro: "Tootsie means never having to say you're sorry" (mid-run changes to data, optimizer and hyperparameters worked) but also "Tootsie means having to say you're sorry kind of a lot" (many unintentional changes, e.g. Llama-2 rotary settings kept for 2.7T tokens); z-loss is needed for very deep cooldowns; "high quality" data lacks few-shot-inducing formats, FLAN fixes that; EMA monitoring adds little under WSD. From the 32B retro: "Pride goeth before a fall" — skipping QK-Norm "cost us time and compute"; midflight changes can work; shuffle quality matters even with per-domain shuffling; double-check cached configs (GSM8K contamination); instrument heavily. The 2026 process is visibly a reaction: pre-registered forecasts, ladders before hero runs, a canary, and agent-run screening.

## 5. What is and is not reconstructable

Reconstructable from open sources: full recipes (data mixes to 0.1%, LR schedules, batch schedules, hardware per phase, step counts), token counts per phase, final evals, the enumerated Delphi grid, and the identity/outcome of ~500 experiment issues. Not reconstructable without W&B/HF access: loss curves and absolute loss values for 8B/32B, per-phase wall-clock dates for the 8B run (only a schematic timeline image exists; I inferred months from issue/commit dates), token counts for the 13B/24B/70B trials, the Delphi α, the 535B forecast loss, and Snowball's config/hardware. The lab has published no statement of total compute or of the experiment-vs-final split; a rough derived floor for released dense runs is 6.1e23 (8B) + 1.2e24 (32B) + ~2.1e23 discarded 32B cooldown, and the Delphi suite is ~1.4e23. For the 535B run the lab's own figure is ~1% of compute on the ladder.

## Update 2026-09-13: losses pulled from W&B

`marin-community/marin` is public and readable anonymously through W&B's GraphQL API; the `stanford-mercury/marin` project the 8B retrospective links is private. Run mapping is pinned in `src/fetch_wandb.py`; curves in `data/losses/marin/`. `loss` in runs.csv = `eval/paloma/c4_en/loss` at the run's last step (nats/token, Llama 3 tokenizer).
